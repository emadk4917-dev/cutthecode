# CONTRACT: the Claude Code prompt that finds the traps in a contract

Free · Cut The Code · checked 26 Nov 2026

**What this is:** the exact prompt we gave Claude Code, the 8 traps it found in a made-up freelance contract (worst first, with clause numbers), and the polite reply it wrote asking for 3 changes.

**Our run:** a made-up £2,400 website contract. In 43 seconds Claude ranked **8 traps**, worst first: no limit on what you could owe (4.1), the client can walk away and pay nothing (5.2, 5.3), unlimited revisions (1.2), and the sneaky one: you only invoice on completion (2.2), but revisions run "until the Client is satisfied" (1.2), so the site is never finished and you never get paid.

## 1. The prompt (from the video)

Save the contract as contract.md (or put the PDF in the folder), open Claude Code there, and paste:

> Read contract.md, a freelance contract I've been sent. Find every clause that could cost me money or time, rank them worst first, quote the exact clause number, and write the 3 changes I should ask for, as a short polite reply, in reply.md. Plain English.

## 2. The 8 traps to check in any contract

1. **Unlimited liability:** "any loss… including loss of profits". Ask for a cap at the fee.
2. **Walk-away clause:** they can end it and owe nothing for work done. Ask to be paid for work done so far.
3. **Unlimited revisions:** "until the Client is satisfied". Ask for 2 rounds, then an hourly rate.
4. **Your toolkit becomes theirs:** pre-existing templates and code. Keep them; give a licence.
5. **Free support + auto-renewal:** support with no end and no fee. Price it separately.
6. **Non-compete:** a ban on a whole industry. Ask to remove it.
7. **Slow or held-back pay:** "90 days of the end of the month", disputes with no time limit. Ask for 30 days.
8. **One-sided caps:** their liability capped at £100. Make it apply both ways, or not to your fees.

## 3. Follow-up

> Add the support and non-compete asks to reply.md as a second, optional paragraph.

## The honest catch

- **It's not a lawyer.** Claude said so itself: a good starting point, not legal advice. For a big contract, have a solicitor read it.
- The contract in the video is a made-up sample. No real company or person.
